COMPLEX INPATIENT CARE

Juliana

Product strategy + platform direction

A platform strategy that strengthens hospital teams.

The opportunity

Complex inpatient care calls for patient identification, shared care planning and consistent operations. The product needs to support existing hospital teams and fit how care is delivered.

My role and decisions

I led discovery and the Juliana IQ strategy around Find, Understand and Act. I connected the founders’ Operate/Enable care-delivery models with product strategy and platform architecture, and directed a synthetic-data demonstration. This work helped position the company around strengthening existing hospital teams.

What changed

The work brought the product direction and company positioning together around a collaborative partner that strengthens hospital teams. It gave the team a concrete demonstration for commercial conversations.

Progress

Leadership approved the positioning. The work is in commercial discovery; a synthetic-data demonstration is not evidence of clinical deployment or new product outcomes.

What it demonstrates

Connecting care-model understanding, company positioning and platform choices before scaling the product.

INCUBATION → LAUNCH

Glimmer Health

Incubation + product strategy; now Strategic Advisor to the CEO

A care model becomes an implementable venture.

The opportunity

UPMC’s specialist chronic-pain model had clinical depth, but extending it meant working through delivery constraints, specialist capacity and health-system complexity. Primary-care physicians needed a trusted care partner, not simply another recommendation tool.

My role and decisions

I led about 18 months of discovery and product strategy inside UPMC Enterprises. I developed the company thesis, patient and physician journeys, staged care model, platform direction and economic rationale. With UPMC clinical leaders and Redesign Health, I worked through validation milestones and enterprise adoption requirements.

What changed

The key choice was a virtual, clinician-led care model with a path to technology-enabled services. Care delivery, workflow and commercial design had to develop together. The founding leadership team took responsibility for building and operating the company; my role evolved from incubation and product strategy to advising the CEO.

Outcome

Glimmer Health publicly launched with Redesign Health in May 2025. Since July 2026, I have served as Strategic Advisor to the CEO on commercial strategy, business development, operating priorities and financing.

What it demonstrates

Venture incubation that starts with a care-delivery problem and carries through to company formation, with a deliberate transition from builder to advisor.

COMMERCIALIZATION → ACQUISITION

Safar Telecare

Product + commercialization strategy

Making specialist access a repeatable enterprise product.

The opportunity

UPMC’s inpatient teleconsult technology addressed the friction of initiating and routing urgent specialist consultations. The commercial opportunity was to carry that workflow advantage into a repeatable product across hospitals.

My role and decisions

I led commercialization strategy across ICU, stroke, neurology and psychiatry. Working with product, engineering, clinical and operations leaders, I unified requirements for intake, routing, documentation and specialist connectivity. We focused on high-value service lines and deferred capabilities that did not strengthen the core workflow.

What changed

I evaluated standalone and partnership options, then helped shape the product, buyer story and transaction pathway with eVisit. The strategic-partnership path offered a way to extend the platform beyond the constraints of standalone growth. My work evolved toward governance and escalation as partner teams took on integration and scale.

Outcome

Safar Telecare was acquired by eVisit in 2024. The platform supported 40,000+ consultations and a reported 92% reduction in specialist connection time. These are collective platform outcomes reflecting the work of clinical, product, engineering and operations teams, not solely my results.

What it demonstrates

Commercializing a health-system asset requires choices about product scope, distribution and partnership, then continuity through the transition to new ownership.

UNDERWRITING → CLINICAL ADOPTION

BrainCheck

Product + commercial underwriting; implementation; board observer

The investment thesis had to work in the clinical workflow.

The opportunity

Earlier identification of cognitive decline requires more than an assessment tool. Screening needs to connect with clinical interpretation, documentation, patient communication and referral inside an already busy care setting.

My role and decisions

I contributed to UPMC Enterprises’ Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias (ADRD) strategy and market assessment, identified BrainCheck as a strategic partner, and developed product and commercial underwriting for its Series B. My contribution supported the investment process; it was not sole investment authorization.

What changed

I then led implementation with clinicians in perioperative and primary-care settings. We worked through screening, Epic documentation, patient communication, stratification and referral. The staged approach connected the investment thesis and enterprise requirements with frontline use. Screening, documentation and referral had to form a workable care pathway, and training helped address adoption barriers.

Outcome

The work connected Series B diligence with clinical deployment and board-observer involvement. I brought a health-system buyer and product perspective to discussions of strategy, commercialization and enterprise adoption.

What it demonstrates

Investment judgment and implementation are connected disciplines. Carrying a thesis into practice exposes assumptions that diligence alone cannot resolve.
